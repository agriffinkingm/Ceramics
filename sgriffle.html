How Sgriffle works

Scrabble, except the only playable words are names: every surname in the 2000 US Census held by at least 1,000 people, plus every first name the Social Security Administration has recorded for at least 1,000 babies since 1880 — about 37,000 names in all, including a pass of famous mononyms, bands and initials (Oprah, Sting, Lizzo, Picasso, Pelé as Pele, Radiohead, ABBA, AOC, JFK, RBG, MLK, DMX, RDJ, GRRM…) and nicknames — tap a played tile to see who any initials belong to — plus a few house names: Grief, Graff, Gruff, Gryph, Groff, Gorf, Garf, Grr, Gr, Grif, Sgriff, Sgriffle, Kojo, Lenk and Maho. Smith counts, Li counts, Antonacci counts, Brunilda counts. "Table" does not.

17×17 board, 9 tiles on your rack, 129 tiles in the bag (2 blanks) — and everyone starts the game holding a blank of their own. No centre rule: the first name goes anywhere, at least two letters — though the centre square is a triple name on every board, so there's a reason to go there. After that, every play must touch the names already down, and every run of letters it makes must be a name.

First turn is up for grabs. In a shared game nobody goes first by default — whoever lays down a name first takes the first turn, and the other player goes next.

Tap a tile then a square, or drag it there; drag tiles within the rack to rearrange them. You can lay tiles out on the board while you wait for your turn — only the play button waits. If the other player covers one of your squares, that tile hops back to your rack.

Build-off bonus. Extend a name already on the board — add letters to either end, or bridge two — and the new, longer name earns its plain letter value again on top (any name you form that contains two or more old tiles counts). It's added, never multiplied, so it can't stack with the name squares.

Bonus squares are shuffled every game, chess960-style: the game number sets the layout (mirrored four ways). double letter · triple letter · double name · triple name. Bonus squares count only for the tiles you just placed.

Clocks. A timed game gives each player a bank of minutes; every move you make adds the increment back. The default is 10 minutes with 10 seconds a move. Run out and you lose on time, whatever the score. Correspondence games have no clock — play a move whenever, the link keeps the game. In a shared game the clock starts with the first name played.

Sgriffle wins. Play the name SGRIFFLE and the game is over — you win, whatever the score. (There are five G's and four F's in the bag to make it thinkable.)

All nine tiles in one go wins the game. Empty your rack in a single play and it's over, whatever the score. You can exchange tiles instead of playing while the bag has at least nine left (that uses your turn). The game ends when the bag is empty and someone has played their last tile (they collect the value of the opponent's rack; the other subtracts it), or after six passes in a row.

When a game ends, analyze the game steps through every move with the rack that player held and shows the highest-scoring play that was available — points-only, no defence or lookahead.

Not sure a name counts? The is it a name? box under the board checks the list and shows the names that start or end with what you typed — handy for seeing what a chain could become. Invalid plays simply bounce — there's no challenging. Play from different phones (make a game and send the link), on one phone, or against the computer: hard always takes its best-scoring play, normal picks from its top six, easy plays from the weaker half of what it sees.
